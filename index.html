Welcome to El Nido

Palawan

What is this place?

El Nido is a Philippine municipality on Palawan island. It’s known for white-sand beaches, coral reefs, and as the gateway to the Bacuit archipelago, a group of majestic islands featuring dramatic steep karst cliffs.

Discover the Wonders

Immerse yourself in crystal clear turquoise lagoons, secret hidden beaches, and world-class diving spots across Palawan's finest waters.

  • 45+ Islands & Islets
  • 100+ Coral Species
  • 98% Pristine Waters

Endangered Wildlife & Flora

Protecting the rare and vulnerable endemic animals and plants native to Palawan.

Endangered Animals

Vulnerable

Palawan Hornbill

Anthracoceros marchei

A threatened endemic bird species known for its distinctive casque and loud calling habits in native lowland forests.

Critically Endangered

Philippine Pangolin

Manis culionensis

Also known as the Palawan pangolin, this solitary nocturnal mammal feeds primarily on ants and termites.

Endangered

Green Sea Turtle

Chelonia mydas

A large marine turtle that frequently nests on the protected sandy beaches and feeds in the coral reefs around El Nido.

Vulnerable

Peacock-Pheasant

Polyplectron napoleonis

A medium-sized pheasant recognized by its striking iridescent blue-green crest and eye-spots on its tail feathers.

Endangered Plants

Critically Endangered

Attenborough's Pitcher Plant

Nepenthes attenboroughii

A giant carnivorous pitcher plant endemic to Palawan’s ultramafic mountain slopes, capable of holding up to 1.5 liters of fluid.

Vulnerable

Palawan Cherry (Balayong)

Cassia nodosa

Famous for its stunning pink-and-white seasonal blooms, this native tree is a cultural symbol of Palawan’s biodiversity.

Critically Endangered

Palawan Slipper Orchid

Paphiopedilum fowliei

A rare, ground-dwelling orchid with mottled leaves and delicate blooms, severely threatened by habitat loss and illegal collection.

Endangered

Almaciga (Manila Copal)

Agathis philippinensis

A towering ancient conifer valued for its natural resin, over-harvested in old-growth forests across Palawan.
