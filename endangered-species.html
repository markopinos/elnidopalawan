Protecting the Wildlife & Flora of El Nido, Palawan

Guardians of Palawan’s Biodiversity

El Nido’s limestone forests, mangroves, and coral reefs shelter rare wildlife and endemic plants classified under the IUCN Red List. Explore the species below by conservation status.

IUCN Status • VU

Vulnerable Species

Species facing a high risk of extinction in the wild due to shrinking forest habitats and human disturbance.

Vulnerable

Palawan Peacock-Pheasant (Tandikan)

Polyplectron napoleonis

Habitat: Humid lowland forest floors of El Nido

A striking ground-dwelling bird endemic to Palawan, celebrated for the male’s iridescent blue-green plumage, white eyebrow stripes, and shimmering tail eyespots.

  • Endemic to the island of Palawan
  • Featured as an official cultural emblem of Palawan heritage
  • Threatened by deforestation and illegal capture for the pet trade
Vulnerable

Palawan Binturong (Bearcat)

Arctictis binturong whitei

Habitat: Dense forest canopies of northern Palawan

An arboreal mammal known for its thick black fur, tufted ears, and muscular prehensile tail, playing a vital role as a seed disperser in El Nido’s forests.

  • Uses a muscular prehensile tail like a fifth limb when climbing
  • Disperses strangler fig seeds essential for forest regeneration
  • Population declining due to shrinking primary forest cover
IUCN Status • EN

Endangered Species

Species facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild and requiring active habitat and marine protection.

Endangered

Green Sea Turtle (Pawikan)

Chelonia mydas

Habitat: Seagrass beds & coastal waters of Bacuit Bay

A large marine turtle that grazes on El Nido’s underwater seagrass meadows and returns to quiet white-sand beaches across Bacuit Bay to nest.

  • Keeps seagrass beds healthy and productive for reef fish
  • Frequently spotted gliding over shallow reefs around Miniloc and Dilumacad
  • Threatened by marine plastic ingestion, boat propellers, and habitat loss
Endangered

Calamian Hog Deer

Axis calamianensis

Habitat: Forest edges & grasslands of northern Palawan

A compact, elusive deer species known for its low head-down running posture through thick underbrush and its highly restricted island range.

  • Found only in the Calamianes and northern Palawan island group
  • Active at dawn and dusk, sheltering in dense forest cover by day
  • Threatened by agricultural expansion and illegal hunting
IUCN Status • CR

Critically Endangered Species

Species facing an extremely high risk of extinction in the immediate future without strict conservation enforcement.

Critically Endangered

Philippine Pangolin (Balintong)

Manis culionensis

Habitat: Lowland forests & mangroves of Palawan

Covered in overlapping protective keratin scales, this solitary nocturnal mammal feeds exclusively on ants and termites and is the most heavily trafficked mammal in the region.

  • Found exclusively in the Palawan faunal region
  • Curls into a tight armored ball when threatened
  • Severely threatened by illegal wildlife trafficking and habitat loss
Critically Endangered

Hawksbill Sea Turtle (Pawikan)

Eretmochelys imbricata

Habitat: Coral reefs & nesting beaches of Bacuit Bay

Recognized by its narrow pointed beak and patterned amber shell, this sea turtle plays a crucial role in keeping El Nido’s coral reefs healthy by feeding on sea sponges.

  • Nests on quiet white-sand beaches across El Nido
  • Maintains reef balance by controlling sponge populations
  • Threatened by illegal shell trade, plastic pollution, and warming seas
Endemic Flora

Endangered Plants of Palawan

Rare botanical treasures native to Palawan’s limestone cliffs and ultramafic mountain forests, threatened by habitat loss and illegal collection.

Critically Endangered

Attenborough’s Pitcher Plant

Nepenthes attenboroughii

Habitat: High-altitude ultramafic mountain slopes of Palawan

One of the largest carnivorous pitcher plants in the world, producing massive bell-shaped pitchers capable of holding up to 1.5 liters of digestive fluid.

  • Endemic to a single mountain range in Palawan
  • Traps insects and small invertebrates to survive in nutrient-poor soil
  • Severely threatened by illegal plant poaching and climate shifts
Endangered

Almaciga (Manila Copal)

Agathis philippinensis

Habitat: Old-growth lowland & montane rainforests

A towering ancient conifer prized for its aromatic natural resin (copal), which is traditionally harvested by indigenous communities for varnishes and incense.

  • Can grow over 60 meters tall in undisturbed Palawan forests
  • Provides vital canopy shelter for endemic hornbills and mammals
  • Threatened by over-tapping of resin and illegal logging
Critically Endangered

Palawan Lady’s Slipper Orchid

Paphiopedilum fowliei

Habitat: Shaded limestone & mossy forest floors

A rare ground-dwelling orchid distinguished by its dark mottled leaves and delicate pouch-shaped blossoms adapted to shaded karst environments.

  • Strictly endemic to Palawan’s forest ecosystems
  • Pollinated by hoverflies lured into its slipper-shaped labellum
  • Listed under CITES Appendix I due to extreme poaching pressure

How Visitors Can Protect Wildlife

Keep a Safe Distance

Never touch, feed, or chase sea turtles, birds, or forest animals while exploring.

Protect Nesting Shores

Keep beaches free of trash and bright flashlights at night so pawikan hatchlings can reach the sea.

Zero Wildlife Trade

Never buy exotic pets, wild orchids, or souvenirs made from shells, corals, or forest products.
